# Q-001: What `search --adjacent` shows as a BNS section's heading

Status: pending
Slug: slice-5
Raised by: wf-tickets and wf-build, ticket 3 (search adjacent sections)
Blocking: no
Reversible: yes

## Question

The spec says each `adjacent` neighbour carries its "heading line". IPC sections have a real marginal heading. The BNS text we parse has none for most sections (251 of 358 have no em dash in the first line), so for those the "heading" is the opening words of the section body. What should a client see for a BNS neighbour?

## Why only you

This is about what the tool promises its users, and the spec does not say whether a body excerpt is acceptable where no title exists.

## Options

1. Keep the current rule: first line of the section text cut at the first em dash, capped at 100 characters. Every neighbour has a string, but for most BNS sections it reads as a truncated sentence rather than a title.
2. Return `heading: null` when the section has no real heading (no em dash in the first line). Clients never see body text dressed up as a title, but most BNS neighbours show only a Citation.
3. Add proper BNS headings by parsing them from the source (for example the bold runs, as `ipc.py` does for IPC). Best output, but it is parser work in a later slice and needs a `PARSER_VERSION` bump.

## Default taken

Option 1, in `neighbours()` in `src/ibid/index.py`, returned as `{"citation", "heading"}` in `previous` and `next` by `src/ibid/mcp.py`.

## If you answer differently

Option 2: change one expression in `neighbours()` and the ticket 3 tests that assert on BNS heading strings. Option 3: a new parser ticket for BNS headings plus a `PARSER_VERSION` bump; the `adjacent` payload shape stays the same.
